## SC-011 — every image says what it is

`images-are-described.test.ts` enumerates every `Image` TAG, not every file, and was **red
against the shipped product in three places**: the notification thumbnail, the compose slot
and the media-picker tile all announced themselves as "image". Two are decoration and are
hidden; the third is labelled by the description field beside it.

It refuses `accessibilityIgnoresInvertColors` as an answer — that is about colour inversion
and says nothing about whether an image is described. Accepting a mechanism instead of
measuring the thing is what let a 36.7pt touch target pass under a comment claiming 44.

## What Phase C found, and where

- **REMOVING A REPORTED COMMENT DID NOTHING TO THE COMMENT.** `report.service.ts` has
  accepted `subjectType: 'comment'` since 001; a moderator's removal transitioned the report
  and wrote the audit log while the comment stayed on the page. Reporting existed and
  removal did not, on a Constitution IV release gate. The id could not locate the row
  either — the check was a regex over a bare ULID under a comment claiming "moderation
  resolves the full item when reviewing", and a comment lives in its post's partition under
  a sort key carrying `createdAt`. A fabricated id was accepted too, in the same test file
  whose next case says reporting something that does not exist is refused rather than
  silently queued.
- **A MENTION ANNOUNCED AT PUBLISH TIME IS ONE NOBODY EVER RECEIVES.** A post is `pending`
  until its media is processed and the boundary shows a non-ready post only to its author,
  so `canOpen` refuses — and the event fires once. On the real path, where transcoding takes
  seconds, the person named would never have been told. Found by a test that published and
  then polled, which is the shape a device takes.
- **Three images announced themselves as "image"** (above).
- `surface-routing.spec.ts` went red on a new constructor argument — the third time that
  file has caught a stub agreeing with an older shape.
