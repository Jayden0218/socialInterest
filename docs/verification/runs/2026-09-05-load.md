## Attribution

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| bottleneck | `datastore` |

**Evidence.** Throughput is flat at 6–7 req/s across every level while latency
rises in direct proportion to concurrency. That is the signature of a fully
saturated dependency: adding callers adds queueing, not work done. A system
limited by its own fan-in arithmetic would show throughput continuing to climb
until CPU ran out.

`bench:ceiling` measured the three candidate limits separately on the same box:

| Subject | Peak req/s |
|---|---|
| Load generator | 187,439 |
| **DynamoDB Local** | **827** |
| Application shape, datastore stubbed | 5,574 |

The emulator is the binding constraint by roughly 7×. The arithmetic agrees: at
~7 feed requests per second with the 200-follow cap, the feed issues on the order
of 1,400 datastore operations per second, which is past the 827 req/s DynamoDB
Local sustains — and the fan-in queries are heavier than the `DescribeTable`
calls used to measure that ceiling.

## What this does and does not establish

**Does**: the end-to-end path works over HTTP under concurrency with zero errors,
and the feed is comfortable at rest (p50 183 ms at concurrency 1 with a
200-interest fan-in over 100,000 posts).

**Does not**: anything about whether read-time fan-in meets `002/SC-002` at
10,000 concurrent readers. This run is a measurement of DynamoDB Local. Feature
001 published p95 11.8s at 100 concurrent and read it as a fact about the
architecture; it was not, and neither is this.

`002/SC-002` remains **unverified**. Closing it requires T052 — a run against
provisioned DynamoDB — which is gated on the project owner's approval to spend.
